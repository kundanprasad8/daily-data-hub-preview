Where Does Delhi's Pollution Come From?

The biggest drivers of pollution in the Delhi-NCR region are well documented. From October to November, smoke from crop-residue burning in Punjab and Haryana drifts southeast into the capital region. Year-round, exhaust from millions of vehicles, dust from construction sites, and emissions from small industries add a steady background load of PM2.5 and PM10.

Local factors make it worse: winter temperature inversion traps this mix close to the ground, and low wind speeds in December and January let particles accumulate for days. Festival fireworks in October-November add short, sharp spikes on top of the seasonal baseline.

Delhi's pollution has been studied more than any other Indian city's. Monitoring by the Central Pollution Control Board shows winter PM2.5 levels among the highest of any world capital, driven by the October-November stubble season, year-round vehicle exhaust from over 10 million registered vehicles, construction dust, and winter inversion. The Graded Response Action Plan (GRAP) triggers emergency measures — from dust control to vehicle restrictions — when AQI crosses set thresholds.

Seasonal Patterns: When Is Delhi's Air Worst?

October is when Delhi-NCR's pollution season begins. Post-monsoon calm sets in, stubble fires start in the northwest, and the first inversions appear. November is usually the worst month, with multi-day Severe episodes. December and January stay bad but steadier, February improves as winds pick up, and March brings real relief. July-September monsoon months are the cleanest of the year.

Today's reading sits in this seasonal context: early in the season, day-to-day values swing with wind direction — a breezy day can halve the AQI, while a calm day after stubble burning can double it.

October is a turning-point month for air quality across much of India. The monsoon has withdrawn, winds weaken, and nights grow cooler — the exact conditions that let particles accumulate near the ground. In Delhi, today's reading of 162 should be read against this seasonal shift: values that look poor now often climb further into November and December in the northern and central regions, while coastal and southern cities typically stay steadier through the retreating monsoon.

What is PM2.5 and why does it matter?
PM2.5 refers to fine particles under 2.5 micrometers wide. They penetrate deep into the lungs and enter the bloodstream, which is why they drive most of the health risk in AQI calculations and why doctors track them more closely than coarser dust.
How is AQI calculated?
Monitoring stations measure pollutant concentrations continuously, convert each pollutant to a sub-index using breakpoint tables, and the highest sub-index becomes the headline AQI. In Indian cities PM2.5 or PM10 usually sets the number.
Why does AQI change through the day?
Pollution peaks in the early morning when cool, still air traps emissions near the ground, then eases toward afternoon as the sun warms the air and winds pick up. Evening traffic brings a second, smaller peak. A single daily number averages across these swings.
Does rain improve air quality?
Yes — rain is the most effective natural air cleaner. A heavy shower can cut particulate levels dramatically within hours by washing particles out of the atmosphere. That is why monsoon months record the cleanest AQI of the year across India.
Are indoor air purifiers useful?
On Poor-or-worse days, a HEPA purifier in the bedroom measurably lowers indoor PM2.5, which matters because people spend most of their time inside. Keep windows closed while it runs, and size the purifier to the room for real effect.
When is air quality worst during the day?
Typically early morning (6-9 AM) and late evening, when cooler air traps pollutants near the ground. Midday to afternoon is often the cleanest window for outdoor activity.
How can I protect myself from air pollution?
Use N95 masks outdoors on poor-air days, run air purifiers indoors, keep windows closed during peak pollution hours, avoid exercising near traffic, and check the daily AQI before planning outdoor time.
