Where Mumbai sits — and why its weather behaves this way

Mumbai lies in Maharashtra, in what climatologists would call a coastal climate regime. That single fact explains most of what you see on this page: the timing of the hot months, when the rains arrive, how cold the winter gets, and even why humidity feels the way it does on a given afternoon. Mumbai's weather is not random — it follows the rhythm of the Indian monsoon system, the same vast circulation that decides the fate of the kharif crop across the subcontinent.

Because Mumbai sits in a coastal zone, its year divides naturally into three acts: a hot summer, a monsoon, and a winter. The exact months and intensity differ from, say, a coastal metro or a Himalayan town — which is why a generic 'India weather' forecast is never as useful as this Mumbai-specific one. Everything below is tuned to Mumbai's geography, not to Delhi's or Mumbai's.

Summer in Mumbai: what to expect

Summer in Mumbai runs roughly March to June and is hot and humid, with daytime highs of 32–38°C and humidity often above 70%. Sea breezes give some relief in the evenings, but nights stay warm and sticky.

For daily life in Mumbai, summer means planning around the heat: schools adjust timings, construction crews start at dawn, and anyone travelling should carry water and cover their head between 11 AM and 4 PM. The hottest weeks are also when power demand peaks, so brief outages are more common — charge devices overnight if you depend on them.

Monsoon in Mumbai: the season that matters most

The monsoon reaches Mumbai around June to September and is heavy and persistent. Mumbai receives intense spells that can flood low-lying streets within hours. This is when the city gets the bulk of its annual rainfall.

For Mumbai, the monsoon forecast on this page deserves more attention than any other season's. A 70% rain probability in July means something very different from a 70% probability in January — in monsoon months it usually means a genuine washout, while in winter it may mean only passing drizzle. Watch the day-to-day trend in the 7-day table above rather than any single number.

Winter in Mumbai: the cool months

Winter in Mumbai spans December to February and is the most pleasant window — mild days of 22–30°C, lower humidity and cool sea breezes. Tourist footfall peaks in these months.

Winter is also when Mumbai's air quality is typically at its worst — cold air traps pollution near the ground, especially on calm, foggy mornings. If you are sensitive to dust or smoke, winter mornings are the time to check the AQI alongside this temperature forecast.

Humidity in Mumbai: the invisible factor

Humidity is the most misunderstood number on this page. At 36% right now, Mumbai is showing a fairly typical value for its coastal setting — but 'typical' covers a wide range through the year. In peak summer the same city can swing from bone-dry afternoons to muggy monsoon nights above 90%.

High humidity does three practical things in Mumbai: it makes heat feel worse (the 'feels like' temperature), it makes rain more likely when clouds build, and it makes everything — clothes, walls, stored grain — slower to dry. If you are drying laundry or planning outdoor paintwork in Mumbai, low-humidity winter afternoons are your friend, not monsoon weeks.

Best time to visit Mumbai

If you are planning a trip to Mumbai, the winter months described above are almost always the sweet spot: comfortable days, clear skies and the lowest chance of disruptive weather. Summer visits are possible but demand heat discipline — early-morning sightseeing, midday rest, and evening outings.

Monsoon travel to Mumbai is a gamble with a payoff: the landscape is at its greenest and crowds are thinnest, but transport delays are common and some outdoor attractions close. Health and safety tips for Mumbai's current conditions

With Mumbai currently at 36°C and clear sky skies, the standard advice applies: hydrate before you feel thirsty, especially if the humidity is high and sweat is not cooling you efficiently. Children, the elderly and outdoor workers in Mumbai are the most vulnerable to both heat and cold extremes.

Second, if rain is in the forecast, remember that the first monsoon showers turn Mumbai's roads slick with months of accumulated oil and dust; drive slower than you think you need to.
