Bitcoin — Complete Guide

What is Bitcoin?

Bitcoin (BTC) is the world's first decentralised digital currency, introduced in 2009 by the pseudonymous creator Satoshi Nakamoto. It runs on a peer-to-peer network with no central bank or single administrator — transactions are verified by network participants and recorded on a public ledger called the blockchain. Bitcoin is the largest cryptocurrency by market capitalisation and is widely regarded as digital gold: a scarce, borderless store of value.

Bitcoin's supply is hard-capped at 21 million coins, written into its code. New bitcoins are created through mining, where powerful computers compete to validate transactions and secure the network. Roughly every four years, the block reward is cut in half in an event called the halving, which slows new supply and has historically preceded major price cycles.

How Does Bitcoin Work?

Bitcoin works through a distributed ledger maintained by thousands of independent nodes worldwide. When someone sends bitcoin, the transaction is broadcast to the network, grouped into a block with other transactions, and confirmed by miners using proof-of-work — an energy-intensive computation that makes the ledger extremely expensive to attack or rewrite. Once confirmed, the transaction is effectively irreversible.

Ownership of bitcoin is controlled by private keys — long secret codes that prove you own the coins at a given address. Lose the private key and the bitcoin is gone forever, which is why Indian investors typically hold BTC on regulated exchanges or in hardware wallets rather than managing keys themselves.

Bitcoin vs Alternatives

Compared with altcoins, Bitcoin is the most battle-tested network: it has never been hacked at the protocol level in over 15 years, has the deepest liquidity, and is the only crypto held as a treasury asset by public companies and nation-states. Ethereum offers programmability that Bitcoin deliberately avoids; stablecoins like USDT offer price stability that Bitcoin does not. Investors often treat BTC as the core holding and altcoins as higher-risk satellites.

How to Buy Bitcoin in India

Buying Bitcoin (BTC) in India is straightforward: create an account on a crypto exchange operating in India (popular options include CoinDCX, CoinSwitch, ZebPay and WazirX), complete KYC with your PAN and Aadhaar-linked mobile number, deposit rupees via UPI or bank transfer, and place a buy order for BTC. Tax rules you must know: India taxes crypto gains at a flat 30% (plus surcharge and cess), with no deduction except the cost of acquisition — and losses cannot be set off against other income. Exchanges operating in India deduct this automatically, but you remain responsible for correct reporting in your income-tax return. Safety tips: enable two-factor authentication, beware of fake exchange apps and 'double your crypto' scams on social media, and consider moving large holdings to a hardware wallet rather than leaving everything on an exchange. Never share your seed phrase with anyone.

Bitcoin Tokenomics: Supply Explained

Tokenomics — the economics of a coin's supply — is one of the most important fundamentals to understand. Bitcoin (BTC) currently has a circulating supply of 20,097,368 BTC tokens trading in the market. Its maximum supply is capped at 21,000,000 BTC, meaning no more than this many tokens can ever exist — a hard scarcity guarantee written into the protocol.

Why does supply matter? The price of any asset is set where supply meets demand. A coin with a fixed, capped supply (like Bitcoin's 21 million) cannot be debased by creating more of it — scarcity is enforced by code. Coins with uncapped or inflationary supplies need constantly growing demand just to hold their price. When evaluating BTC, always look at fully diluted valuation (price × maximum possible supply), not just market cap — it shows what you are really paying if all tokens ever created entered circulation.

Also watch vesting schedules: many newer tokens allocate large shares to teams and investors that release over time, creating predictable sell pressure. A high fully-diluted valuation relative to current market cap is a warning sign that significant supply is still waiting to hit the market.

People Also Ask

Is Bitcoin legal in India?

Bitcoin trading is not banned in India. It is legal to buy, sell and hold, but gains are taxed at a flat 30% plus 1% TDS on transfers. The RBI has expressed concerns about crypto in the past, but no ban is in force.

What is Bitcoin halving?

Roughly every four years (every 210,000 blocks), the reward miners earn for validating transactions is cut in half. This slows the creation of new bitcoin and enforces its 21-million supply cap. Halvings in 2012, 2016, 2020 and 2024 each preceded major bull markets historically.

Can I buy less than one Bitcoin in India?

Yes — Bitcoin is divisible to 8 decimal places (the smallest unit is called a satoshi). Indian exchanges let you start with as little as ₹100, so you can own a tiny fraction of one BTC.

What is the 21 million supply cap?

Bitcoin's code limits total supply to 21 million coins, expected to be reached around 2140. This fixed scarcity — unlike fiat money that central banks can print — is the core of Bitcoin's 'digital gold' investment thesis.
