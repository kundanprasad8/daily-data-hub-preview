Understanding 360 ONE Balanced Hybrid Fund NAV — A Complete Guide

What is NAV?

NAV stands for Net Asset Value — the per-unit price of a mutual fund scheme. When you invest in 360 ONE Balanced Hybrid Fund, you are allotted units at the applicable NAV; when you redeem, you receive the NAV prevailing on that business day (minus any exit load, if applicable). The NAV of 360 ONE Balanced Hybrid Fund is declared once every business day after market close, as mandated by SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India). Unlike a stock price, which changes every second during market hours, a mutual fund NAV is a single end-of-day snapshot.

How is the NAV of 360 ONE Balanced Hybrid Fund calculated?

The formula is the same for every mutual fund in India:

NAV = (Market value of all securities + receivables − liabilities − expenses) ÷ total outstanding units

For 360 ONE Balanced Hybrid Fund, 360 ONE's fund accounting team totals up the closing market value of every security the scheme holds, adds accrued income and receivables, subtracts liabilities and the day's accrued expenses (including the management fee), and divides by the number of units outstanding. The result is rounded to four decimal places — which is why you see NAVs like ₹Direct Plan precision figures on this page. Cut-off timings matter: purchase or redemption requests received before the SEBI-prescribed cut-off get the same day's NAV; requests after the cut-off get the next business day's NAV.

Why does the NAV of 360 ONE Balanced Hybrid Fund change every day?

As a hybrid fund, the NAV of 360 ONE Balanced Hybrid Fund reflects a blend of equity and debt market moves. The equity portion drives most of the day-to-day fluctuation, while the debt portion cushions sharp falls. The exact mix depends on the fund's mandate and the manager's current allocation.

Three forces move the NAV daily: (1) market movement in the underlying securities, (2) income accrual (dividends received and interest earned get added), and (3) expense accrual (the fund's daily operating costs are deducted). Net inflows and outflows from investors change the unit count but not the NAV itself — the NAV is purely a function of portfolio value divided by units.

Direct plan NAVs are always slightly higher than Regular plan NAVs of the same scheme because Direct plans do not pay distributor commission, so less is deducted as expenses each day. Over years, that small daily difference compounds into a meaningful return gap.

Direct vs Regular: which variant of 360 ONE Balanced Hybrid Fund should you pick?

If you invest without an intermediary (directly through the AMC website or a direct-plan platform), the Direct plan of 360 ONE Balanced Hybrid Fund gives you the same portfolio at a lower cost. If you rely on a distributor or advisor for guidance, you will be placed in the Regular plan, and the commission is built into its slightly lower NAV trajectory. Neither variant is "better" in portfolio terms — the holdings are identical. The choice is about whether you pay for advice embedded in the plan or separately.

Growth vs IDCW option in 360 ONE Balanced Hybrid Fund

In the Growth option, all profits stay inside the scheme and compound — the NAV reflects the full accumulated gains. In the IDCW (Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal) option, the fund may periodically distribute surplus to investors, and the NAV drops by the distribution amount on payout. For long-term wealth creation, Growth is usually the default choice; IDCW suits investors who want periodic cash flows and understand that distributions are not guaranteed and are subject to tax.

The expense ratio effect on 360 ONE Balanced Hybrid Fund NAV

Every day, a tiny slice of 360 ONE Balanced Hybrid Fund's assets — the expense ratio divided by 365 — is deducted before the NAV is struck. A difference of even 1% in annual expense ratio, compounded over a decade, can change your final corpus by a double-digit percentage. This is why the Direct vs Regular decision above matters so much: it is the single biggest controllable driver of your long-term outcome in 360 ONE Balanced Hybrid Fund, bigger than trying to time entries and exits.

SIP investing in 360 ONE Balanced Hybrid Fund: practical tips

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) in 360 ONE Balanced Hybrid Fund buys more units when the NAV is low and fewer when it is high — this is rupee-cost averaging, and it removes the need to predict NAV movements. Practical pointers: (1) pick an amount you can sustain for years, not the maximum you can afford this month; (2) align the SIP date with your salary credit so it never bounces; (3) review 360 ONE Balanced Hybrid Fund once a year against its benchmark and category peers, not every week against its own NAV; (4) increase the SIP by 10% each year if your income grows (step-up SIP); (5) do not stop SIPs when the NAV falls — Hybrid funds are less volatile than pure equity funds but can still fall meaningfully when equity markets correct.

Key takeaway

The NAV of 360 ONE Balanced Hybrid Fund is a daily scorecard, not a buy/sell signal by itself. A rising NAV feels good and a falling NAV feels bad, but neither tells you whether the fund is well-managed. Judge 360 ONE Balanced Hybrid Fund on rolling returns versus its benchmark, consistency across market cycles, portfolio quality, and cost — and use this page's daily NAV as the raw input for that judgement, updated every business day from AMFI's official data.
